# Paper review rubric

A paper can be merged when the answer to all required checks is **yes**. Uncertainty should produce a request for clarification, not an automatic rejection.

## Required checks

1. **Traceable research** — Is there a stable paper page, DOI, proceedings entry, or preprint?
2. **LLM relevance** — Is an LLM a meaningful part of the research question, method, evaluation, or application?
3. **Psychology relevance** — Does the work use, test, operationalize, critique, or extend a psychological theory or construct?
4. **Grounded mapping** — Is the relationship more precise than a shared word or loose human analogy?
5. **Claim–evidence fit** — Does the submitted description avoid inferring a human mechanism, capacity, or experience from behavior alone?
6. **Metadata quality** — Are title, authors, year, venue, URL, citation key, and map connections accurate?
7. **Ethical and population boundaries** — Where relevant, are user impact, cultural scope, sampling, and structural limitations acknowledged?

## Review labels

- `accepted`: passes the required checks and is ready to merge.
- `needs-grounding`: promising, but the construct or mapping is underspecified.
- `needs-metadata`: fit is clear, but the record is incomplete or inaccurate.
- `out-of-scope`: does not meaningfully connect psychology and LLM research.
- `duplicate`: already represented by an existing record.

## Important cautions

- A benchmark score does not by itself establish a psychological capacity.
- A module named attention, memory, reflection, or personality does not by itself instantiate the psychological construct.
- Popularity is not validity. Disputed instruments and frameworks can be included when the paper is relevant, but the controversy should not be hidden.
- Negative and critical results are eligible. The map should not become a catalogue of only successful analogies.
